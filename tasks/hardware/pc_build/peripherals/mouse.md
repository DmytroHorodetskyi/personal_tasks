## The characteristics that matter, and how to read them

| # | Spec (unit) | What it means, plainly | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Length and grip width (mm), against hand size** | Mouse length, and the width at the narrowest point where thumb and ring finger squeeze. Compared against hand length (wrist crease to middle-finger tip) and knuckle width. | **The spec every reviewer puts first**, and it is the one this file used to leave out. A good sensor in the wrong-sized shell is an expensive mistake. See "Fit" below. |
| 2 | **Grip style** | Palm (whole hand down), claw (palm anchored, fingers arched), fingertip (palm off). Most people are a hybrid. | Decides which of the two measurements binds: palm grip needs length, claw grip needs grip width. |
| 3 | **Weight (g)** | Under ~60 g is "lightweight" now; ~100 g is heavy by 2026 standards. | Revised: not just an aim spec. Reviewers tie heavy mice to wrist and finger strain **over long sessions**, which is what this machine is mostly doing. |
| 4 | **Button count** | How many programmable buttons beyond left/right/wheel. | A **work** spec, not a gaming one. Thumb buttons take IDE and terminal shortcuts — but see the caution below: reviewers report buying many-button mice and never using them. |
| 5 | **Scroll wheel: free-spin** | A ratcheted wheel clicks per line; a free-spin wheel releases and coasts. | Scrolls a 4 000-line file in one flick. Only the G502 family here has it. |
| 6 | **Wired / wireless** | 2.4G wireless is indistinguishable from wired in latency now. | A desk mouse never travels, so wireless buys tidiness and costs either a charging habit or an AA. |
| 7 | **Battery** | AA cell vs internal rechargeable. | AA runs ~250 h and is swapped in seconds; rechargeable runs ~140 h and needs a cable on the desk anyway. |
| 8 | **Sensor** | Pass/fail, not a ranking. **PixArt 3311, 3330, 3370 or 3395 — or any current Logitech HERO / Razer Focus — is fine.** | Every candidate below clears the bar. **Ignore the DPI number**; 25 600 DPI is marketing and nobody uses above ~3 200. |
| 9 | **Onboard memory vs software** | Whether button assignments live in the mouse or need G HUB / Synapse running. | Only matters once buttons are remapped for work. Vendor software failing to load is a reported nuisance. |

**Ignore:** DPI ceilings, polling rates above 1 000 Hz, RGB, switch-rating click counts, and
"premium" shell materials (carbon fibre, magnesium) — all four are marketing.

## Fit: the two measurements, and what they rule out

Added 2026-09-11 after eight buyer's guides were checked (sources at the bottom). Five of the
eight open on this and treat everything else as secondary.

**How to measure** — do it once, it takes a minute:

- **Hand length.** Ruler flat on the desk, zero at the *wrist crease* (the line the hand bends
  on, not the base of the palm — starting at the palm costs a centimetre and sends you a size
  too small). Measure to the tip of the middle finger. Hand flat and relaxed, not stretched.
- **Hand width.** Across the knuckles, outside of index to outside of pinky. Rocket Jump Ninja
  includes the thumb knuckle; Kimmy excludes it. Take both numbers.

**How to read them** — two independent rules that agree:

| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| Rocket Jump Ninja (21 years of FPS reviews) | Mouse dimensions ≈ **60%** of hand measurements; mouse length ≈ length of the middle finger back to the knuckle. Grip width is the measurement that decides aim; length decides comfort. |
| Kimmy (40+ mice tested) | Hand under 17.5 cm = small, 17.5–19 cm = medium, 19 cm+ = large. Medium hands → mouse length ~115–125 mm. Hand width under 8 cm → grip width 53–58 mm; ~8–9 cm → 57–60 mm; 9 cm+ → 62–64 mm. |

Tools both name: **rocketjumpninja.com/mouse-search** (takes hand length + width + grip type,
returns mice with their length and grip width), **eloshapes.com** (2D outline overlays, misses
curvature), **rtings.com** (3D scans, smaller database, partly paywalled now).

**Also from the guides, and it applies whatever gets bought:** a new shape feels wrong for the
first days. Give it **two to three weeks** before judging. Unfamiliar is adaptation; pain,
numbness or cramping after two weeks is the wrong mouse.

## What eight buyer's guides agree on

Checked 2026-09-11: Rocket Jump Ninja ×2, DiamondLobby ×2, Kimmy, Hardware Canucks, Linus Tech
Tips, GamingScan.

- **Shape and fit beat every spec on the box.** Unanimous among the seven current ones.
- **Weight: lighter, and it is a comfort argument, not only an aim one.** DiamondLobby's
  ceiling for shooters was **~85 g in 2024 and under 55–65 g by 2026**; Rocket Jump Ninja says
  under 60 g; Hardware Canucks says anything under 80 g feels much the same and balance matters
  more than the number. All three tie heavy mice to wrist and arm fatigue over long sessions.
  **This revises row 3 of the spec table above** — the earlier "near-irrelevant for eight hours
  of cursor work" was the wrong way round.
- **Many-button mice: buy them for a reason, not for the count.** Hardware Canucks' fourth
  "mistake" is his own: bought an MMO-style mouse to move keyboard shortcuts onto it, never used
  the side buttons, went back to a comfortable shape — and notes those bodies run large.
  DiamondLobby is blunter, and specifically about the G502: *"the less people using a G502 for
  long hour gaming the better"*, on wrist and finger strain. Neither is a measurement, and both
  are one reviewer's hands. But the 13-button, 89–106 g G502 is the exact shape both are
  warning about, so it does not get picked here by default.
- **Sensor and polling rate are settled.** Any current sensor is fine; above 1 000 Hz is a
  gimmick (Hardware Canucks cites Battle(non)sense's measurements; RJN cannot see it on a
  high-speed camera).
- **Wireless 2.4G is not a compromise.** Six sources agree. Only GamingScan (2019) says
  otherwise, and it also argues optical-vs-laser — it is six years stale and discounted here.
- **The price ceiling is lower than this file's top end.** DiamondLobby: optimal **$90–125**,
  and do not exceed **$130** for a gaming mouse. Three candidates below sit above that.
- **Cheap is no longer a warning sign — for specific brands.** DiamondLobby 2026 calls ATK /
  VXE R1 Pro, VXE MAD R, MCHOSE L7 Pro and A7 Ultra "endgame level" at $45–65. LTT's lab put
  the $40 Metaphyuni Panda P1 Pro as the sweet spot — good accuracy, stable latency — while the
  $20 Attack Shark R1 showed jitter and **10.8 ms** latency against **sub-2 ms** for the
  $100 DeathAdder V3 HyperSpeed. Both warn that warranty on these means shipping to China.
  **None of these brands is in the candidate table below, and none has been price-checked in
  Ukraine** — see open question 2.

## What the table says

- **The fit filter kills three of the ten, including the former front-runner.** The whole G502
  family is 131.4 mm long and 79.2 mm wide against a 116.6 mm mouse that already suits the
  hand — 15 mm longer and 17 mm wider. That is not a preference call any more.
- **Two candidates are the shell already in use.** The G305 is the G102/G203 shell to within
  0.05 mm. Buying it is buying the current mouse without a cable.
- **The G305 at 2 220 is Foxtrot-only.** Telemart wants 3 299 for the identical black SKU —
  a 1 079 spread on one product, the widest in this file.
- **The G305 is heavier than the mouse it replaces**: 99 g with the AA against 85 g for the
  G102. The G305 X Superlight is the same shape at 59 g — the only candidate that keeps the
  known-good shape *and* moves weight the way every reviewer says it should go.
- **Everything above 3 700 is a different, larger shape**, so the top half of the price list
  buys a change of hand position rather than a better mouse.

## Reviews of the two finalists

Checked 2026-09-11, after the verdict was set on specs and price alone.

**Razer Cobra — good, not universally loved. 63% positive** across 51 aggregated Reddit
reports (19 positive, 7 negative, 4 neutral). The complaints are specific and worth knowing:

- **Scroll wheel and side buttons are the weak parts.** One enthusiast scores them 4/10 and
  5/10. This is the complaint that lands on the work half of the usage, not the gaming half.
- **Coating is plain textured plastic**, and the rubberised side grip is not replaceable — the
  first thing expected to wear.
- **Shape suits fingertip and claw, not palm grip.** Not a problem against a G102, which is the
  same class of small mouse, but it decides the matter if the grip is palm.
- **Synapse 4 runs ~450 MB of RAM** resident, and the mouse stores **one onboard profile**.

Against that, TechPowerUp — the most instrumented of the sources — is positive: no rattle, no
shell creak under lateral force, no accidental side-button actuation, 57 g measured, low
lift-off distance, flexible cable, and a verdict of a proper successor to the Viper Mini.
**The rigorous review is positive; the negative reports are mostly aggregate user sentiment and
generic Razer-software gripes.**

**Logitech G305 X Superlight — reviewed well, criticised on price and feet.** PCGamesN: "by far
the best G305 yet", tempered by the price rise, and notes Logitech does not state the main
switch brand or its rated lifespan. PC Gamer: solid, but the price bump stops it being a
no-brainer against the old G305 on sale. Recurring across reviews: too small for large hands or
palm grip (not an issue here), barely visible RGB, and one flagged flaw in the skates.

**What this does to the decision: nothing decisive.** Neither mouse has a reliability problem
that shows up in measurement. If the scroll wheel is the part that gets used hardest — long
files, all day — that is the one argument for paying the extra 1 129 for the Logitech.

## What happens if the scroll wheel does play up

The drawbacks are not to be ignored; they are to be sorted into two piles, because only one of
them has a remedy.

| | Remedy |
|---|---|
| **Defect** — wheel skips, jumps or scrolls the wrong way; double-clicks; button stops registering | Warranty. **24 months from the manufacturer** and **14 days for a no-argument exchange**, as stated on Telemart's own listing. Razer has authorised service centres in Ukraine. |
| **Design** — the wheel feels vague or cheap, the coating is plain plastic, the side grip wears | No remedy. This is what the 4/10 score describes, and no warranty covers "I don't like it". Lived with, or sold on. |

The practical consequence is a **plan, not a gamble**:

1. **Buy from a shop with a service department** — Telemart, Foxtrot or ITbox, all at 2 499,
   not the 1 859 hotline seller. The 640 difference *is* the warranty being easy to claim.
   This is the same rule as [../price_check.md](../price_check.md).
2. **Hammer the wheel in the first fourteen days.** Long files, all day, deliberately. That
   window overlaps the two-to-three weeks the reviewers say a new shape needs anyway, so the
   adaptation period and the exchange period are spent on the same test.
3. **If it misbehaves after that**, try firmware and a Synapse reinstall first — a jumping wheel
   is often software — then claim through the shop, which is inside the 24 months.

The exposure, stated plainly: **2 499 UAH and a fortnight's attention**, against a 1 129 saving
over the Logitech. If that trade is unappealing, the Logitech is the answer and the reason is
not the specs — it is that its weak points are the skates and the price, not the wheel.

## The verdict

Revised once, the same day, after the candidate field was widened past Logitech G. The first
verdict (G305 X Superlight, 3 628) stood on a list seeded from four Logitech G queries and one
Razer; eleven further makes were priced afterwards and one of them is a better answer. Recorded
2026-09-11.

**Razer Cobra, 2 499 UAH — Telemart, ITbox or Foxtrot, identical price, in stock.**
119.6 × 62.5 × 38.1 mm against the G102's 116.6 × 62.2 × 38.2: three millimetres longer, the
same width and the same height, so the hand position that already works does not change. 58 g
against 85 g. Wired, like the mouse being replaced, which is not a complaint on record. Six
buttons, the number in use today. Its dimensions are published, which the G305 X Superlight's
are not.

**If wireless is wanted: Logitech G305 X Superlight, 3 628** — the exact G305 shell, 59 g,
rechargeable. It costs 1 129 more than the Cobra, and that money buys the missing cable.

**Cheaper: HATOR Quasar 3 Wireless, 2 199** — 64 g and wireless for 300 less than the Cobra,
but 125 mm long, at the top of the fit band and 8 mm longer than the shape known to work.
Cheapest thing in the file that is not a compromise on the sensor.

**Not recommended at any price:** the G502 family (131.4 × 79.2 mm — outside the band), the
MX Master 3S (84.3 mm wide, 141 g), and everything above 4 000, which buys a different shape
rather than a better mouse.
