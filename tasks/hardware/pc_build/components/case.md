# Case

## What the case does for this machine

It sets the ceiling on how cool and quiet everything else can be, and it is the part you
physically interact with - front ports, dust filters, how painful the build and later
upgrades are. It contributes no performance, but a bad case turns good components into loud
ones: a GPU starved of intake air spins its fans up, and then you hear it all day.

Two honest notes: cases are the most taste-driven purchase in the build, and above ~5 000
UAH you are buying materials and looks, not thermals.

## The characteristics that matter, and how to read them

The case decides noise and temperature far more than most people expect, and almost all of
that comes from the first two rows.

| # | Spec (unit) | What it means, plainly | What it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Front panel type** | Mesh lets air in. Solid glass or a "designer" front does not - it starves everything behind it. | **Mesh front.** This single choice outweighs every fan upgrade you could buy on top of it. It is why two good-looking cases on the list are rejected. |
| 2 | **Included fans: count and size (mm)** | Bought separately these are 300-500 each. Size matters as much as count: **a bigger fan moves the same air at a lower RPM**, which is the whole trick to a quiet machine. | The Lancool 216 includes **2x 160mm front + 1x 140mm rear** - unusually large, roughly 1 200 of real value. One 6 460 case on the list includes **zero**. |
| 3 | **GPU clearance (mm)** | Maximum card length. | RX 9070 XT models run 280-350mm; the Hellhound is **327mm**. The Lancool 216 takes **392mm**. Every case listed fits. |
| 4 | **CPU cooler clearance (mm)** | Maximum tower height. | Needs **160mm+** for the Phantom Spirit's 154mm. The Lancool 216 allows **180.5mm**. |
| 5 | **Airflow path** | Front and bottom intake to rear and top exhaust, without the PSU shroud or a glass panel in the way. | **Bottom intake matters specifically for the GPU**, which sits above it and pulls its air downward. |
| 6 | **Dust filters** | Front, top and bottom mesh, ideally removable without tools. | You will clean them every few months. Magnetic filters make that a 30-second job instead of a disassembly. |
| 7 | **Front I/O** | USB-A count, and whether there is **USB-C** - which needs a matching header on the board. | USB-C on the front is worth having. Check the board has the header, see [motherboard.md](motherboard.md). |
| 8 | **Build quality and cable management** | Steel thickness, depth of the rear cable channel, velcro straps, PSU shroud. | This is what the price above 4 000 actually buys - not looks. |
| 9 | **Radiator support (mm)** | Where a 240/360 AIO would mount. | Irrelevant unless you overrule the air-cooling verdict in [cooling.md](cooling.md). |
| 10 | **Size** | ATX mid-tower vs mATX. | **ATX mid-tower**: cheaper per feature, easier to build in, quieter. |

**Ignore:** tempered glass, RGB, "gaming" styling, and claimed fan-mount counts you will never
populate. A case that needs 1 500 of fans added to work is not cheaper than one that does not.

## Evidence (revised after the deep review pass, 2026-09-09)

- **Lancool 216 - confirmed, and my pick survives.** Gamers Nexus has it as the
  **out-of-box CPU thermal co-leader** in its 2025 roundup and a **noise-normalised GPU die
  temperature co-leader** in 2024. In GN's own review of the newer 217, the 216 essentially
  matched it (40 C all-core, 43 C GPU); the 217 only pulls ahead at impractical maximum fan
  speeds. The two 160mm intakes are the reason: a bigger fan moves the same air at lower RPM,
  and noise scales steeply with RPM. Its quirk: those 160/170mm fans are integral and
  unsuited to radiators.
- **Lancool 217** measured slightly better still (41 C all-core, 42 C GPU at a normalised
  27 dBA, and GN's VRM/DDR5 leader) but regresses in ways that matter: its front-panel
  buttons are **hardwired power-only**, it needs a **PSU of 150mm or less** in rotated mode,
  and the metal under the glass is thin.
- **Montech AIR 903 MAX - now an avoid.** Thermally strong (56 C GPU, VRM 10 C cooler than
  rivals) but acoustically wrong for this build: **48.5 dBA maximum**, and the reviewer
  documented **fan-speed oscillation between 40-49% PWM** - audible hunting in exactly the
  range a quiet desk-side machine operates in.
- **Phanteks XT Pro Ultra - deprioritised.** Gamers Nexus's verdict is "merely fine",
  mechanically and thermally unremarkable with a measurable gap to cheaper Lancools. It also
  has **no removable front fan filter**. The four 140mm fans are a marketing hook the airflow
  result does not follow.
- **NZXT H5 Flow (2024) - the quiet surprise.** Tom's Hardware measured its fans **below the
  36.4 dBA noise floor of their meter**. Downside: limited storage, and distinguish it from
  the 2022 model sharing the name.
- **Deepcool CC560 V2 - unverified.** No measured data from Gamers Nexus, TechPowerUp or
  Tom's. Not a judgement, but you are buying on reputation rather than numbers.
- **be quiet! Pure Base 501 Airflow - unverified in this pass.** Reviews exist (Overclock3D,
  HWCooling, Igor's Lab) but all blocked automated access. Likely good for a quiet build;
  worth a dedicated look if silence is your priority.
- **The one case that beats the 216 on evidence: Fractal Meshify 3, 7 350-8 405.** Gamers
  Nexus's **best overall case** and its **noise-normalised CPU leader at 44 C**.
- GN's explicit avoid list, worth knowing: **Fractal Epoch** (a rebadged North with a cheaper
  front panel, worse than a Lancool 207) and the **Corsair 6500 series** (bendy unreinforced
  panels, misalignment).

## Verdict (revised)

**Lian Li Lancool 216, 4 667** - unchanged, and now backed by Gamers Nexus's noise-normalised
data rather than my assumption. Three fans included, two of them 160mm, no extra fan purchase
needed, and easy to build in.

**Better but pricier: Fractal Meshify 3, from 7 350** (+2 683). It is the measured leader -
GN's best overall and its noise-normalised CPU champion. If you want the quietest machine the
budget can produce, this is where the case money goes.

**Cheaper: Deepcool CC560 V2, 2 280** (-2 400). Mesh front, four fans, well-liked - but
understand that no lab has measured it. **Or the NZXT H5 Flow (2024) at 3 479**, which *has*
been measured as extremely quiet, if you can live with less storage room.

**Avoid: Montech AIR 903 MAX** (48.5 dBA and PWM hunting in your operating band) and
**Phanteks XT Pro Ultra** (unremarkable, no front filter). And still: no glass-front or
zero-fan designer case at this budget.

## Requirement restated by Dmytro (2026-09-11): thermals and noise only

**Looks, RGB, glass, cable-management aesthetics and brand do not count.** The case is judged on
airflow, measured noise, included-fan value and clearance - nothing else. Everything in this
document about tempered glass, PSU shrouds and lighting is therefore informational only and must
not influence a pick.

## Cross-check against five case guides (2026-09-11)

Sources: Tech Guided *How to Choose A PC Case* (2024-01), CORSAIR/Linus *How to choose your next
case* (2019, vendor), Mr Matt Lee *Case Sizes Explained* (2025-03), **PC Builder *Best PC Case
2026*** (2026-03) and **GeekaWhat *Best PC Cases to Buy Right Now*** (2025-09).

**None of the five contains a thermal or noise measurement.** They are buying guides, and this
was re-confirmed by reading all five end to end on 2026-09-13. This document rests on **Gamers
Nexus noise-normalised data**, which outranks all five, and the CC560 V2's lack of a lab test is
not closed by anything in them.

**But one of them endorses the pick, and that was left out (added 2026-09-13).** The
US-market cases listed here before - Thermaltake V170, Okonos Aqua 3/7, Montech XR, Gamdias
Atlas M1 - are PC Builder's *budget and mid-range* picks. His premium section says: **"my
favorite front intake PC case of all time, and that is the Lancool 216."** And his reason is the
stated criterion rather than looks: *"The two fans in the front are 160 mm ARGB ... You can spin
these fans much slower and get similar air flow to for instance 3 120 millimeter fans and they're
almost silent."* That is an airflow-per-noise argument for exactly the two 160mm intakes in the
table below. It does not change the pick - it supports it.

What they do add is a set of checkable rules, applied here:

### One mismatch this pass found, unrelated to cooling

**The CC560 V2 has no front USB-C.** Its front I/O is **1x USB 3.0 + 1x USB 2.0 + audio**.
The Lean build's ASRock B850M Pro RS WiFi carries a front-panel USB-C header, which
[motherboard.md](motherboard.md) calls "the thing people forget" - and in the Lean build it will
have nothing to plug into. Not a thermal issue and not a reason to change the case on the stated
requirement, but it should be a known consequence rather than a surprise at assembly.

**Clearances re-verified against Deepcool's spec:** CC560 V2 takes a **165mm cooler and a 370mm
GPU**, against a 155mm AK400 (or the far smaller bundled Wraith Stealth) and a ~300mm 9070 GRE.
Wide margin on both.

(2024) at 3 479** (+1 199). Five buying guides cannot close that gap, because none of them
measures anything. Only a lab test can.
