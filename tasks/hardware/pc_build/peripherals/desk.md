## The characteristics that matter, and how to read them

| # | Spec (unit) | What it means, plainly | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Width (cm)** | Usable top, left to right. | Set by the room. 120-140 cm per the requirement. |
| 2 | **Depth (cm)** | Front edge to back edge — 60 or 70 on almost every desk sold here. | **The spec the requirement did not name and the one that decides the monitor.** At 60 cm a 27" panel sits roughly 50 cm from the eyes, which is too close at 1440p. 70 cm is the first depth that works without a monitor arm pushing the panel back past the edge. |
| 3 | **Height (cm)** | 73-75 fixed is standard. Adjustable models quote a range. | Fixed per the requirement. |
| 4 | **Load rating (kg)** | What the top carries. | Only matters if a monitor arm gets clamped on later; none of these publish it. |
| 5 | **Top material** | Laminated chipboard on every desk in this price band. | Not a differentiator below ~10 000. |
| 6 | **Frame** | Steel tube vs panel sides. | Decides wobble when typing hard. Not published; a shop-floor check, not a spec-sheet one. |

**Ignore:** RGB edge lighting, "carbon fibre texture" tops, headphone hooks and cup holders.

## What the table says

- **Only one desk in the size band has 70 cm of depth: the GT Racer T-1443 at 5 999.**
  Everything else at 120-140 cm is 60 cm deep. Given that the build's monitor is a 27" 1440p
  panel ([../components/monitor.md](../components/monitor.md)), this is the single most
  consequential row in the file.
- **The cheap end is unbuyable as data.** Xtrike ME and GamePro desks at 3 599-4 999 publish
  no dimensions at all in any of the four shops' listings — they cannot be compared to the
  requirement without a shop-floor visit or a manufacturer page.
- **Rozetka's cheap GT Racer prices are single marketplace sellers.** T-1444 at 4 050 against
  6 071-6 990 at three component shops for the same desk; T-1443 at 5 400 against Foxtrot's
  first-party 5 999. Those are one seller's numbers, not the market's.
- **A 140 cm sit-stand is 6 599** — 600 more than the fixed 140x70. Ruled out by the
  requirement; recorded because the category normally costs ~6 000 more, not 600. The cost is
  depth: 60 cm, not 70.

From the listing: 138 cm is specified as fitting two 27" monitors; 82 cm of depth holds monitor,
mat and legroom with the desk against a wall; the front legs adjust for tilt; two cable
pass-throughs are placed at the outer edges so they do not foul a low-sensitivity mouse sweep.
Rounded corners, curved front edge. Widths 124 / 138 / 156, and custom sizes to order in 7 days.

**The delivery figures are not the same kind of fact, and this file presented them as if they
were.** The Оптима's 400/435 comes from the **seller's own free-text blurb** in the product
description ("На відділення 400 грн / На адресу додому 435 грн"). Rozetka's own delivery
block on that page quotes no number at all — it says **Тариф перевізника**, the carrier's rate,
settled at the counter. The Brevity's 1 349/1 599, by contrast, is Rozetka's own calculated
quote to Kyiv. So the 3 394-against-3 936 comparison rests on a seller's marketing line against
a retailer's quote, and the gap could narrow or vanish at checkout. **The desk still wins on
depth, price and review count, none of which depend on this.**

**Why this file missed it.** Every pass searched for "140x70" or by loft and gaming brand names,
because 70 cm had been fixed as the target depth in the spec table. **Nothing ever searched for
*more* than 70 cm** — a target was treated as a ceiling. Gaming desks at 80+ cm deep exist at
this price and none of the four passes surfaced one. The lesson for the rest of the file: a
requirement stated as a minimum must not be typed into a search box as an exact figure.

## The verdict

**Стіл Оптима, розмір L, 138 x 82 x 75 cm, чорний — 2 994 UAH**, plus Nova Poshta at the
carrier's rate (the seller's blurb says ~400; Rozetka quotes no figure).
Found by Dmytro, verified here 2026-09-11. It replaces both earlier verdicts.

It is deeper than anything this research produced, inside the 120-140 cm width band, fixed
height, and cheaper delivered than the desk this file recommended an hour earlier.

**The two real drawbacks, neither disqualifying:**

1. **90 days of warranty**, against 12 months on every other candidate. Mitigated by payment on
   delivery — no prepayment — and Rozetka's 14-day return.
2. **A marketplace seller and no brand.** CyberStaff rates 4.5/5 across 132 ratings and the
   product carries 169 reviews, which is more user evidence than every other desk in this file
   combined; but there is no manufacturer behind it if the seller stops trading.

## Open questions — both answered

1. **Is 60 cm depth acceptable?** Treated as no. 60 cm puts a 27" panel roughly 50 cm from the
   eyes once the stand base is subtracted, and the 70 cm option costs 1 300 more than the
   cheapest 60 cm desk while adding width as well. This is the one judgement in the file that
   is a comfort call rather than a measurement — if 60 cm is known to be fine in practice, the
   T-1211 at 4 699 is the answer instead and nothing else changes.
2. **Do the undimensioned budget desks need chasing?** Partly done, and it killed the cheapest:
   **Xtrike ME DK-02 is 110 x 60 cm**, below the stated 120 cm minimum, so the 3 599 entry is
   out on the requirement rather than on missing data. GamePro publishes nothing for the GD210B
   at any of the four shops or its own listings; the sibling GD214 is 136 x 60. Treated as out
   — a desk that will not publish its size cannot be checked against a brief that is entirely
   about size.
