### A. The microphone — the binding requirement

| # | Parameter | What it means | Why it decides this purchase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Noise suppression vs naturalness** | A mic tuned for a natural voice picks up everything in the room; one with compression and gating mutes the room but sounds less open | **The most important row for Dmytro.** He types on a mechanical keyboard *during* calls. The Corsair HS80 sounds like a proper condenser **and picks up every keystroke and mouse click**. The Razer BlackShark V2 Pro and Beyerdynamic MMX 200 mute keystrokes by default |
| 2 | **Mic pattern and position** | Headset mics are omnidirectional or wide cardioid, and sit **close to the keyboard as well as to the mouth**. A boom-arm desk mic is cardioid, aims at the mouth and rejects the sides | The physical reason a separate mic beats a headset mic on a mechanical-keyboard desk, independent of money spent |
| 3 | **Sidetone** | Your own voice piped back into the cups | **Missing from this file entirely.** In a *closed* headset without it you cannot hear yourself and you shout. Reviewers rank it alongside mic quality. Corsair's is useless ("you have to eat the microphone"), the Cloud III has none, Sony's and Audeze's are excellent |
| 4 | **Application compression** | Teams, Meet, Zoom and Discord each re-compress your voice heavily | **Caps how much mic quality is worth paying for.** Differences obvious in a back-to-back test largely vanish through Teams. Discord preserves the most |
| 5 | **Removable / flip-to-mute / retractable** | Whether the boom detaches or flips up | Lets one pair serve calls and music without looking like a headset |
| 6 | **Mic sample rate** | Windows can default the mic channel low | The Cloud III Wireless **defaults its mic to a much lower sample rate**, which sounds worse. Set it by hand after install |

### B. Wearing it for eight hours

| # | Parameter | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Clamping force** | How hard it grips your head | **By far the biggest complaint in headset reviews**, and no manufacturer publishes it. Reviews only. Fixable: stretch it overnight over a box |
| 8 | **Weight is not decisive** | 250-490 g across this class | A 490 g Audeze Maxwell is comfortable because it is balanced; a 250 g Drop H3X is not, because the cups are small and the clamp tight. **Balance and cup size beat the number** |
| 9 | **Ear-pad material** | Pleather seals best and gives the best bass but is the first thing to disintegrate from sweat. Fabric breathes, seals worse. Perforated pleather is the compromise | Eight-hour days, and pads are a consumable |
| 10 | **Pad shape and replaceability** | Oval suits more ear shapes but can pinch the jawline; round needs enough thickness to seal. Most pads are removable | A headset with replaceable pads outlives its pads; third-party pads fix specific models |
| 11 | **Headband type** | Self-adjusting suspension band vs separate size extensions | Suspension bands fail on larger heads, thick hair or hats. **Prefer separate extensions** |
| 12 | **Cable noise** (wired only) | Rustle transmitted up the cable into the cup | **Braided cable is the tell — avoid it.** Rubberised cables are quiet |

### C. Sound

| # | Parameter | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | **Closed vs open back** | Closed seals the cup: isolation, no leakage, the most direct presentation. Open lets air through: wider and more natural, leaks in both directions | Dmytro leans closed, and closed is right here — **an open headphone leaks into a desk microphone and lets the room into a call** |
| 14 | **Stereo beats virtual 7.1** | "7.1" is two drivers plus DSP, not seven speakers | Competitive games already apply their own spatial processing; a second layer smears the cues instead of sharpening them. **Turn surround off** |
| 15 | **Gaming bass tuning masks cues** | Boosted bass sounds impressive in a shop demo | Footsteps and reloads sit in the mids. Boosted low end masks them. Flatter tuning hears more |
| 16 | **Published specs are meaningless** | Two headsets both quoting 50 mm drivers, 20 Hz-20 kHz and 32 ohm sound nothing alike and cost wildly different | **Ignore driver size and frequency range.** Tuning decides. Sensitivity is the one number worth reading — higher gives finer volume control |
| 17 | **ANC costs sound quality** | Noise cancelling forces the signal through the headphone's own DAC | Which is why high-performance headphones omit it. Not needed at a desk |

### Why the V2 Pro is rejected outright

Not an expectation mismatch — **a structural defect**. Eleven of thirty reviews describe the
same failure in the same words: the plastic side rails where the yoke meets the cups **crack,
then snap**, typically at six to twelve months, in both Black and White. *"Cracks appeared on
both sides, then during a normal gaming session one cup simply fell off."* One owner was
refused warranty — the service centre claimed he had repaired it himself — and **Razer's own
support told him the sellers are responsible and it was probably a bad batch.**

What owners praise is exactly what the mic test found: sound, **microphone**, battery, fabric
pads. The chassis holding it together is what fails. No price makes that worth buying.

### Why the HyperSpeed is not tarred with it — and it held up

**A different, lighter chassis — and zero breakage reports across its reviews.** Owners
independently confirm the property this whole file turns on:

> *"It cuts out extraneous noise, the person you are talking to barely hears what is around
> you. Weak sounds it cuts 100%."* — and *"the mic is not position-dependent"*, unlike headsets
> that must be eaten to work.

Also reported: **70 hours** of battery ("6 days"), **simultaneous Bluetooth and 2.4 GHz**,
strong range (ten metres through two walls), nothing creaks or has play, no head pressure after
a couple of hours, braided charging cable, EQ presets.

**Its real drawback, named by several owners: the microphone does not detach.** It sticks out
permanently, so the headset cannot double as plain headphones. Two other single reports: one
unit returned for broken positional audio (other owners attribute it to game mode or stale
firmware), and one slow reconnect when switching devices.

### The value option: V2 X, wired

**4.1/5 across 370 reviews** — 191 five-star, 27 one-star. Balanced TriForce drivers without a
bass skew, comfortable over glasses, practical matte finish, **microphone good on PC through
Synapse**. Against it: non-detachable mic, narrow soundstage, thin plastic inside the cups that
large ears contact, and a wire frame some owners call fragile. *(Part of this summary is
Rozetka's own AI-generated digest of open sources — weaker evidence than the individual
reviews, and flagged as such.)*

**Buy it first-party.** A visible share of its one- and two-star reviews are marketplace
sellers' faults, not the product's: resold units with already-registered codes, broken seals,
the wrong colour shipped, sellers who stop replying.

### Evidence corrections this file made along the way

Recorded because three separate claims were asserted here before being checked:

1. **"7 012 at Rozetka, in stock"** — read off a search row. The product page said **8 999,
   out of stock**. A search row is not an offer.
2. **"SlashGear confirms the glasses problem"** — **it does not.** The article never mentions
   glasses, seals or bass bleed; that came from a search-engine summary attributing a claim to
   a source that does not contain it. The glasses finding rests on **RTINGS alone**, and 59
   Reddit owners report no glasses complaints.
3. **"Wireless inconsistency, confirmed by two sources"** — RTINGS' "inconsistencies in audio
   delivery" is almost certainly their *Frequency Response Consistency* metric, i.e. the same
   seal finding restated, not a second problem. The Razer Insider threads are a **support
   forum — a complaint-selected sample**. Against 59 general owner reports saying connectivity
   is reliable and no dongle problems occur, the claim does not stand.

**The pattern in all three: a source was cited before it was read.** Same failure as the
hotline floors in [../price_check.md](../price_check.md).

**A fourth pattern, found 2026-09-13 by the source audit — the scepticism runs one way.** Item 3
above discounts the Razer Insider threads as *"a support forum — a complaint-selected sample"*, and
that is right. But the same objection applies to the **59 positive Reddit owner reports per
headset** that this file's final tie rests on, and it is not made. The Headphone Show, which this
file treats as the authority on research method, states it plainly:

> *"Every single forum thread on a product on Head-Fi has purchase justification in it … it turns
> into support groups for product owners. Take the recommendations from people in product threads
> with a grain of salt."*

Both samples are self-selected in opposite directions. **The tie does not move** — both headsets
are measured by RTINGS and both come out good — but the 71%-versus-71% figure is softer evidence
than it reads as, and it should not be the thing that decides anything.

### The verdict

**SteelSeries Arctis Nova 5 — 6 799 at Telemart or ITbox**, or 5 895 from Rozetka's EU channel
if a week's wait is acceptable.

It wins on the two things that recur across an eight-hour day with glasses: a **retractable
boom** instead of one permanently in front of the face, and a **soft suspension headband that
owners specifically credit with preventing headaches**. Its microphone is the one endorsed by a
measuring outlet **for conference calls by name**, which is Dmytro's stated use. It is also 200
cheaper locally and 15 g lighter.

**Take the HyperSpeed instead if either of these matters:** hearing **phone and PC audio at the
same time** (the Nova 5 cannot), or **81 hours of battery against 60**. Both are real, and
neither is about call quality.

**Not a landslide.** The two tie on owner sentiment, both microphones are good, and no
reliability pattern separates them. Anyone who prefers the Razer on those two features is not
making a mistake.

**Two further corrections, 2026-09-12, both found by Dmytro checking the claim:**

4. **"The Nova 5 cannot work wired."** **False.** It has a 3.5 mm jack and SteelSeries'
   support states it then works as a standard wired headset needing no battery. The USB-C is
   charge-only — but USB-C is not the only port, and this file looked at nothing else.
5. **"Fast charge 15 min = 6 h."** The product description says **8 h**.

So two of the HyperSpeed's listed advantages — a wired fallback and faster charging — **do not
exist**. What remains to it is **81 h against 60 h** and **simultaneous phone + PC audio**.

That is now five claims in this file asserted before being verified. Every one was caught by
Dmytro rather than by the research, and every one had the same cause: a source summarised
rather than read, or a single port, row or page mistaken for the whole picture.
